Past simple vs past perfect; used to

Goal: To narrate past events in the right order using the past simple and the past perfect, and to describe past habits and states with used to.

The idea in two lines: The past simple tells what happened at a finished time in the past. The past perfect goes one step further back: it shows the earlier of two past actions, while used to describes what was true or habitual in the past but is no longer.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Past simple (V + -ed / irregular form): a completed action at a definite past time: The Phoenicians traded across the sea.
  2. Past perfect (had + past participle): an action completed before another past action or time.
  3. Time markers with the past perfect: before, after, by the time, already, just, when, until.
  4. With after the past perfect goes in the earlier action; with before it usually goes in the main clause: They had built walls before the war started.
  5. Used to (used to + base verb): a past habit or state that has stopped: People used to write on papyrus.
  6. Negative and question: didn't use to / Did … use to …? (no -d after did).

🟢 Examples

  1. Join: The empire collapsed. Then the city lost its trade. Solution: After the empire had collapsed, the city lost its trade.
  2. Correct the verb: Ancient Egyptians use to build pyramids. Solution: Ancient Egyptians used to build pyramids (a finished past habit).
  3. Complete: By the time archaeologists (reach) the tomb, thieves (steal) the gold. Solution: reached / had stolen. Explanation: the theft happened first.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Using the past perfect for a single past action with no other past reference.
  • Writing had went instead of the past participle had gone.
  • Writing didn't used to; the correct form is didn't use to.
  • Confusing used to + verb (past habit) with be used to + -ing (be accustomed to).

🟡 Quick review

  • Past simple = finished action at a known past time.
  • Past perfect = had + V3, the earlier past action.
  • After + past perfect, then past simple.
  • Used to = old habit or state, no longer true.
