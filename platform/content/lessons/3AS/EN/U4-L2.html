Quantifiers; cause and effect

Goal: To use quantifiers correctly with countable and uncountable nouns, and to express cause and effect relationships with suitable connectors.

The idea in two lines: Quantifiers tell how much or how many, and the choice depends on whether the noun is countable. Cause-and-effect connectors explain why something happens and what it leads to.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Countable nouns (products, ads): many, few, a few, fewer, a number of.
  2. Uncountable nouns (money, information, advice): much, little, a little, less, a great deal of.
  3. A few / a little = some (positive); few / little = almost none (negative).
  4. Cause + clause: because, as, since; cause + noun: because of, due to, owing to.
  5. Effect verbs: cause → lead to / result in → effect; reversed: effect → result from / be caused by → cause.
  6. Effect connectors: so, therefore, consequently, as a result.

🟢 Examples

  1. Choose: There is too (many / much) advertising on TV. Solution: much. Explanation: advertising is uncountable.
  2. Rewrite with because of: Consumers buy more because ads are attractive. Solution: Consumers buy more because of attractive ads.
  3. Rewrite with result from: Fake products lead to health problems. Solution: Health problems result from fake products.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Writing many informations or an advice; these nouns are uncountable.
  • Putting a clause after because of or due to.
  • Confusing result in (→ effect) with result from (← cause).
  • Using less with plural countable nouns instead of fewer.

🟡 Quick review

  • Many / few / fewer + plural nouns.
  • Much / little / less + uncountable nouns.
  • Because + clause; because of / due to + noun.
  • Cause leads to / results in effect; effect results from cause.
