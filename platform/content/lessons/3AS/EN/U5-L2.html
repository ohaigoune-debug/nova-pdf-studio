Relative clauses and definitions

Goal: To join sentences and write clear definitions using relative pronouns, and to distinguish defining from non-defining relative clauses.

The idea in two lines: A relative clause gives information about a noun just before it. A good definition follows the pattern: term + is a / an + general class + relative clause that states its special feature.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Who for people, which for things and animals, that for both in defining clauses.
  2. Whose shows possession: an astronomer whose discovery changed science.
  3. Where for places, when for time: the year when the probe landed.
  4. Defining clause: essential information, no commas: The planet which is closest to the Sun is Mercury.
  5. Non-defining clause: extra information, between commas, never with that: Jupiter, which is a gas giant, has many moons.
  6. The relative pronoun can be omitted when it is the object of a defining clause: the telescope (that) we used.

🟢 Examples

  1. Join: Galileo was an astronomer. He improved the telescope. Solution: Galileo was an astronomer who improved the telescope.
  2. Define: an observatory. Solution: An observatory is a building where scientists observe the sky.
  3. Join: Saturn has rings. Its rings are made of ice and rock. Solution: Saturn, whose rings are made of ice and rock, … / Saturn has rings which are made of ice and rock. Explanation: its becomes whose.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Repeating the pronoun: the planet which it is red.
  • Using that after a comma in a non-defining clause.
  • Using which for people or who for things.
  • Confusing whose (possession) with who's (who is).

🟡 Quick review

  • Definition = term + is a + class + relative clause.
  • Who / which / that / whose / where / when.
  • Defining: no commas; non-defining: commas, no that.
  • Object pronoun can be dropped in defining clauses.
