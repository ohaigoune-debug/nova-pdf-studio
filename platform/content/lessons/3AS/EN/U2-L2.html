Expressing wish, regret and obligation

Goal: To express wishes about the present and regrets about the past with I wish / If only, and to express obligation and absence of obligation with the right modals.

The idea in two lines: After wish we go one tense back: past for present wishes, past perfect for past regrets. Obligation uses must, have to, should, and past obligation uses had to.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Present wish: I wish / If only + past simple; use were for all persons.
  2. Past regret: I wish / If only + past perfect: I wish I had studied business ethics.
  3. Wish + would: annoyance or desire for change in someone else: I wish they would stop polluting.
  4. Should have + V3 also expresses regret or criticism: They should have told the truth.
  5. Obligation: must (strong, internal or rules), have to (external), should / ought to (moral duty, advice); past: had to.
  6. No obligation: don't have to / needn't; prohibition: mustn't.

🟢 Examples

  1. Rewrite: I'm sorry I didn't report the fraud. Solution: I wish I had reported the fraud.
  2. Rewrite: Workers don't have good salaries. Solution: If only workers had good salaries.
  3. Choose: Employees (mustn't / don't have to) accept bribes. Solution: mustn't. Explanation: it is forbidden, not just unnecessary.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Keeping the same tense after wish: I wish I am rich is wrong.
  • Forgetting to reverse the meaning: a negative fact becomes a positive wish.
  • Confusing mustn't (forbidden) with don't have to (not necessary).
  • Writing musted; the past of must is had to.

🟡 Quick review

  • Wish (now) = past simple / were.
  • Regret (past) = had + V3, or should have + V3.
  • Must / have to = obligation; had to = past obligation.
  • Mustn't = forbidden; don't have to = not necessary.
