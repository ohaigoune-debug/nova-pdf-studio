Expressing purpose, result and concession

Goal: To link ideas logically by expressing purpose, result and concession, and to use the correct structure after each connector.

The idea in two lines: Purpose answers why / what for?, result answers what happened as a consequence?, and concession admits an unexpected contrast. Each connector is followed by a specific structure: a clause, a noun, a gerund or an infinitive.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Purpose + infinitive: to, in order to, so as to (negative: in order not to, so as not to).
  2. Purpose + clause: so that / in order that + subject + can / could / will / would.
  3. Result: so, therefore, consequently, as a result, thus; intensity: so + adj / adv + that, such (a) + adj + noun + that.
  4. Concession + clause: although, though, even though.
  5. Concession + noun or gerund: despite, in spite of; with a clause add the fact that.
  6. However / nevertheless start a new sentence and are followed by a comma.

🟢 Examples

  1. Join with so that: She studies hard. She wants to get a scholarship. Solution: She studies hard so that she can get a scholarship.
  2. Rewrite with despite: Although the school was poor, it got excellent results. Solution: Despite being poor, the school got excellent results.
  3. Join with such … that: It was a difficult exam. Many pupils failed. Solution: It was such a difficult exam that many pupils failed.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Writing despite of; it is despite or in spite of.
  • Putting a full clause after despite without the fact that.
  • Writing for to learn; use to learn or for learning.
  • Mixing so (+ adjective) and such (+ noun phrase).

🟡 Quick review

  • Purpose: to / so as to + verb; so that + clause.
  • Result: so, therefore, as a result; so … that / such … that.
  • Concession: although + clause; despite + noun / -ing.
  • Always check what comes after the connector.
