Writing a short story or anecdote

Objective: To write a short, well-organised story or anecdote using narrative tenses, time sequencers and vivid language that shows feelings.

The idea in two lines: A story moves from a setting to a problem, a climax and an ending. An anecdote is a short, often funny, true-sounding story that ends with a surprise or a punchline.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Structure: opening (characters, place, time), problem, climax, resolution, ending or moral. An anecdote may end with a punchline.
  2. Past simple for the main events in order: He opened the door and screamed.
  3. Past continuous for background actions: It was raining and people were rushing home.
  4. Past perfect for an earlier past action: When I arrived, the bus had already left.
  5. Sequencers: first, then, after that, suddenly, as soon as, while, finally, in the end.
  6. Vivid language: feeling adjectives (terrified, relieved), strong verbs (rushed, whispered) and direct speech in inverted commas.
  7. Usually written in the first or third person, with a short title and paragraphs for each stage.

🟢 Examples

  1. Combine with while: I was reading. The lights went out. → While I was reading, the lights went out. Explanation: long background action + short interrupting action.
  2. Put the verb in the right tense: When we reached the station, the train (leave). Answer: had left (earlier action).
  3. Model anecdote opening: Last summer, my uncle was fishing quietly when, suddenly, his line pulled hard. He was sure he had caught a huge fish… Explanation: setting, background, then a surprise.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Mixing present and past tenses in the same story.
  • Using the past simple for an earlier action instead of the past perfect.
  • Writing one long paragraph with no clear climax or ending.
  • Repeating and then instead of varying sequencers.

🟡 Quick review

  • Setting → problem → climax → resolution → ending.
  • Past simple = events; past continuous = background.
  • Past perfect = the earlier of two past actions.
  • Vary sequencers and show feelings.
  • Anecdote = short story + surprise or punchline.
