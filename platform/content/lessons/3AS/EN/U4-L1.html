Giving advice and warning

Goal: To give advice and warnings to consumers in a polite or firm way, choosing the right modal or structure for each situation.

The idea in two lines: Advice suggests what is good to do; a warning tells someone about a danger and its consequence. Both often appear in consumer-safety texts, leaflets and advertisements.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Advice with modals: should / ought to + base verb; negative shouldn't.
  2. Had better ('d better + base verb): strong advice with a hidden danger; negative had better not.
  3. Other advice forms: If I were you, I would…; Why don't you…?; I advise you to…; It's a good idea to…
  4. Warning with the imperative: Be careful! Watch out! Never… / Don't…
  5. Warning + consequence: imperative + or / otherwise / or else + result.
  6. Unless / if can also warn: Unless you check the label, you may buy fake goods.

🟢 Examples

  1. Give advice: A friend always buys products because of ads. Solution: You should compare prices and read reviews before buying.
  2. Rewrite as a warning: If you don't read the expiry date, you may get ill. Solution: Read the expiry date, otherwise you may get ill.
  3. Choose: You (had better / had better to) keep chemicals away from children. Solution: had better. Explanation: had better takes the base verb without to.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Writing should to or had better to; both take the base verb.
  • Using If I was you in formal writing instead of If I were you.
  • Writing hadn't better; the negative is had better not.
  • Forgetting the consequence after or / otherwise.

🟡 Quick review

  • Advice: should, ought to, If I were you, Why don't you…?
  • Strong advice: had better (not) + verb.
  • Warning: Be careful / Never / Don't…
  • Warning + result: …, or / otherwise + consequence.
