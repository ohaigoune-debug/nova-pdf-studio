The past simple: regular and irregular verbs

Goal: By the end of this lesson, you will be able to narrate the life of a famous person in the past simple, using regular verbs with -ed and the correct forms of irregular verbs in affirmative, negative and interrogative sentences.

The idea in two lines: The past simple describes finished actions at a definite past time (in 1830, last year). Regular verbs add -ed; irregular verbs have a second form to learn by heart (go → went, write → wrote).

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Use: a completed action at a definite past time, with yesterday, ago, in + year, last …. Emir Abdelkader was born in 1808.
  2. Regular verbs: base + -ed: work → worked. Consonant + y: study → studied. One vowel + one consonant: stop → stopped.
  3. Irregular verbs: learn the second column: be → was / were, go → went, write → wrote, become → became, win → won, leave → left, teach → taught.
  4. The past form is the same for all persons: I / he / they wrote. Only be changes: I / he / she / it was; you / we / they were.
  5. Negative: didn't + base form. She didn't win the prize. With be: wasn't / weren't.
  6. Questions: Did + subject + base form? Where did she live? With be: Was he famous? Where were they born?
  7. Pronunciation of -ed: /t/ (worked), /d/ (lived), /ɪd/ after t or d (painted, needed).

🟢 Examples

  1. Put the verb in the past simple: Mouloud Feraoun (write) novels about Kabyle village life. Answer: wrote. Explanation: irregular verb; the past form is wrote, not "writed".
  2. Make the sentence negative: Leonardo da Vinci finished all his paintings. Answer: Leonardo da Vinci didn't finish all his paintings. Explanation: after didn't the verb returns to the base form.
  3. Ask about the underlined words: She moved to Cairo in 1950. Answer: When did she move to Cairo? Explanation: question word + did + subject + base form.

🟠 Common BEM mistakes

  • Double past: "He didn't went", "Did she wrote?" After did, use the base form.
  • Adding -ed to irregular verbs: "goed, teached, becomed".
  • Confusing was and were: "They was born in Tlemcen."
  • Writing the base form in the affirmative with a past time marker: "In 1962 Algeria become independent" instead of became.

🟡 Quick review

  • Finished past action with a definite time → past simple.
  • Regular: -ed (studied, stopped); irregular: learn V2.
  • Negative: didn't + V1; question: Did + S + V1?
  • be: was (I, he, she, it), were (you, we, they).
  • -ed is pronounced /t/, /d/ or /ɪd/ (worked, lived, painted).
