Conditional type 1

Goal: To express a real possibility in the future and its consequence with the first conditional (If + present simple, will + base verb), for example when talking about dreams, studies and ambitions.

The idea in two lines: Conditional type 1 describes something that can really happen. The if-clause gives the condition in the present simple; the main clause gives the result with will (or can, may, an imperative).

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Structure: If + present simple, will + base verb. Example: If it rains, we will stay at home.
  2. Use: a real or likely condition in the present or future and its probable result. It is used for advice, warnings, promises and plans.
  3. Order of clauses: the if-clause can come first (with a comma) or second (no comma): We will stay at home if it rains.
  4. Other results: instead of will, the main clause may use can, may, might, should or an imperative: If you finish early, you can go out. / If you see Karim, tell him to call me.
  5. Unless = if ... not: Unless you hurry, you will miss the bus = If you don't hurry, ...
  6. Negative forms: If he doesn't train, he won't win. Third person singular takes -s or doesn't in the if-clause.
  7. The condition is in the present tense even though it refers to the future: never put will after if.

🟢 Examples

  1. Complete: If Samir ........ (get) good marks, his parents ........ (buy) him a bike. Solution: If Samir gets good marks, his parents will buy him a bike. Explanation: present simple with -s for Samir; will + base verb in the result.
  2. Rewrite with unless: If you don't practise, you won't improve your English. Solution: Unless you practise, you won't improve your English.
  3. Reorder: she / if / a scholarship / she / will / abroad / gets / study. Solution: If she gets a scholarship, she will study abroad. / She will study abroad if she gets a scholarship.

🟠 Common BEM mistakes

  • If it will rain ✗ → If it rains ✓ (no will in the if-clause).
  • Forgetting the -s: If he study ✗ → If he studies ✓.
  • Using unless ... not together: Unless you don't work ✗.
  • Mixing types: present simple in the condition but would in the result (that is type 2).

🟡 Quick review

  • Type 1 = real possibility: If + present simple, will + base verb.
  • Comma only when the if-clause comes first.
  • Result can also use can / may / might / imperative.
  • unless = if ... not.
  • Never if + will.
