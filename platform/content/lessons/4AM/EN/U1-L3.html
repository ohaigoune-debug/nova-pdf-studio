Comparatives and superlatives

Goal: By the end of this lesson, you will be able to compare two landmarks with comparatives (taller than, more famous than) and to pick out one landmark from a group with superlatives (the tallest, the most famous).

The idea in two lines: Short adjectives take -er / the -est (old → older → the oldest); long adjectives take more / the most (beautiful → more beautiful → the most beautiful). A few adjectives are irregular: good, bad, far.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. The comparative compares two things and is followed by than: The Eiffel Tower is taller than Big Ben.
  2. The superlative picks one thing out of three or more and is preceded by the: Mount Everest is the highest mountain in the world.
  3. One-syllable adjectives: add -er / -est (old → older → the oldest). After one vowel + one consonant, double the consonant: big → bigger → the biggest.
  4. Two-syllable adjectives ending in -y: change y to i: busy → busier → the busiest, pretty → prettier → the prettiest.
  5. Longer adjectives: use more / the most: famous → more famous → the most famous; interesting → more interesting → the most interesting.
  6. Irregular forms: good → better → the best; bad → worse → the worst; far → farther / further → the farthest / the furthest.
  7. Equality: as + adjective + as: The Casbah is as famous as the Medina of Tunis. Negative: not as … as.

🟢 Examples

  1. Complete: The Great Wall of China is (long) … than Hadrian's Wall. Answer: longer than. Explanation: long is a one-syllable adjective, so we add -er and keep than.
  2. Complete: Timgad is one of (impressive) … Roman sites in Algeria. Answer: the most impressive. Explanation: impressive has three syllables; superlative = the most + adjective.
  3. Complete: The weather in the Sahara is (bad) … than in Algiers in summer. Answer: worse than. Explanation: bad is irregular; never write "badder" or "more bad".

🟠 Common BEM mistakes

  • Double comparison: "more taller", "the most biggest". Choose -er or more, never both.
  • Forgetting than after a comparative: "Paris is bigger Oran" instead of bigger than Oran.
  • Forgetting the before a superlative: "Everest is highest mountain" instead of the highest mountain.
  • Spelling: "biger", "hotest". Double the final consonant: bigger, hottest.

🟡 Quick review

  • Two things → comparative + than; three or more → the + superlative.
  • Short adjective → -er / -est; long adjective → more / the most.
  • -y → -ier / -iest; big → bigger / biggest.
  • good / better / best; bad / worse / worst; far / farther / farthest.
  • Same degree: as … as; not as … as.
