The present perfect vs the past simple

Goal: By the end of this lesson, you will be able to choose between the present perfect (has written) and the past simple (wrote) when you talk about the achievements of a living or a dead person.

The idea in two lines: The past simple is for a finished action at a definite past time (She wrote her first novel in 1995). The present perfect (have / has + past participle) links the past to now: the time is not given or the period is not finished (She has written ten novels so far).

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Form of the present perfect: have / has + past participle. He has painted. Negative: hasn't / haven't. Question: Has she ever won a prize?
  2. Past simple = definite past time: yesterday, last year, in 2010, ago. The action is over and cut from the present.
  3. Present perfect = indefinite time or unfinished period: ever, never, already, yet, just, so far, this year, since, for.
  4. Since and for: since + starting point (since 2015); for + duration (for ten years).
  5. Living vs dead people: a living artist can take the present perfect (She has published three books); a dead person's life is always in the past simple (Shakespeare wrote many plays).
  6. A typical pair: present perfect first, then details in the past simple. I have visited Timgad. I went there in 2022.

🟢 Examples

  1. Choose: Assia Djebar (write / wrote / has written) her first novel in 1957. Answer: wrote. Explanation: "in 1957" is a definite past time, so past simple.
  2. Choose: Our teacher (read / has read) this biography several times, and she still loves it. Answer: has read. Explanation: no date, and the result continues up to now.
  3. Complete with since or for: Yasmina Khadra has been a writer … more than 30 years. Answer: for. Explanation: "30 years" is a duration; since would need a starting date.

🟠 Common BEM mistakes

  • Present perfect with a past date: "He has died in 1989." A date needs the past simple: He died in 1989.
  • Past simple form after have: "She has wrote" instead of has written.
  • Confusing since and for: "since five years", "for 2010".
  • Present perfect about a dead person: "Mozart has composed" instead of Mozart composed.

🟡 Quick review

  • Past simple: finished action with a definite time (ago, last, in 1990).
  • Present perfect: have / has + PP; time not given or still open.
  • Key words: ever, never, already, yet, just, so far, since, for.
  • since + point in time; for + length of time.
  • Dead person → past simple only.
