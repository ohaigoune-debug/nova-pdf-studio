Relative pronouns: who, which, where

Goal: By the end of this lesson, you will be able to join two sentences with the relative pronouns who, which and where, and to write a definition such as "A museum is a building where works of art are kept."

The idea in two lines: A relative pronoun replaces a repeated noun and links two ideas in one sentence. Who is for people, which for things and animals, and where for places (it means "in that place").

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. A relative clause starts with a relative pronoun and comes right after the noun it describes.
  2. Who refers to people: Ibn Khaldun was a historian who wrote about North Africa.
  3. Which refers to things and animals: The Sphinx is a statue which has the body of a lion.
  4. Where refers to places: Tipaza is a town where you can see Roman ruins.
  5. After who and which, do not repeat he, she, it: a guide who speaks English, not "who he speaks".
  6. After where, the clause has its own subject: the museum where we saw the mosaics. Do not add there at the end.
  7. That can replace who or which (the man that built it), but never where.

🟢 Examples

  1. Join: Gustave Eiffel was an engineer. He designed the Eiffel Tower. Answer: Gustave Eiffel was an engineer who designed the Eiffel Tower. Explanation: "engineer" is a person, so who replaces "He".
  2. Join: The Taj Mahal is a mausoleum. It is made of white marble. Answer: The Taj Mahal is a mausoleum which is made of white marble. Explanation: a thing, so which replaces "It".
  3. Join: Ghardaia is a town. Many tourists visit the old market there. Answer: Ghardaia is a town where many tourists visit the old market. Explanation: a place; "there" disappears because where already means "in that place".

🟠 Common BEM mistakes

  • Using which for people: "the king which built the palace" instead of who.
  • Repeating the subject: "a monument which it attracts visitors". Delete it.
  • Using where when the place is the subject of the verb: "a mosque where is very old". Write a mosque which is very old.
  • Keeping there after where: "the city where I live there".

🟡 Quick review

  • who = people; which = things, animals; where = places.
  • The relative pronoun comes directly after the noun.
  • No he / she / it after who or which; no there after where.
  • If the place is the subject of the verb, use which, not where.
  • BEM definitions: "A … is a person who / a thing which / a place where …".
