Expressing dimensions and quantities

Goal: By the end of this lesson, you will be able to give the size of a landmark (height, length, width, area, weight) and to ask about dimensions and quantities: "How high is it? It is 120 metres high."

The idea in two lines: A dimension is expressed with number + unit + adjective (50 metres long) or the + noun + of (the length of the bridge). A quantity is asked with how many (countable) or how much (uncountable).

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Dimension with an adjective: be + number + unit + adjective. The tower is 120 metres high. The wall is 2 metres thick.
  2. Dimension with a noun: The height of the tower is 120 metres. Learn the pairs: high → height, long → length, wide → width, deep → depth, heavy → weight.
  3. Asking about dimensions: How high / long / wide / deep is it? How much does it weigh? What is its height?
  4. Area and weight: It covers an area of 300 square metres (m2). It weighs 500 tons.
  5. Countable nouns (visitors, steps) take how many, many, a few. Uncountable nouns (water, marble, time) take how much, much, a little. Both take a lot of.
  6. Big numbers: one million visitors; two thousand years old. Million and thousand have no -s after a number.
  7. Approximation: about, nearly, almost, more than, over: The temple is nearly 2,000 years old.

🟢 Examples

  1. Ask the question for the answer: It is 90 metres high. Answer: How high is it? Explanation: How + adjective + be + subject.
  2. Rewrite with a noun: The bridge is 400 metres long. Answer: The length of the bridge is 400 metres. Explanation: long → length, followed by of.
  3. Complete: … visitors come to the site every year? About two … . Answer: How many visitors … About two million. Explanation: "visitors" is countable; million stays singular after a number.

🟠 Common BEM mistakes

  • Mixing adjective and noun: "the high of the tower". Say the height of the tower or it is 120 metres high.
  • Writing "two millions visitors". After a number, million and thousand take no -s.
  • Using how much with countable nouns: "How much steps?" instead of How many steps.
  • Forgetting the unit: "It is 120 high." Always write 120 metres high.

🟡 Quick review

  • is + number + unit + high / long / wide / deep / thick.
  • The height / length / width / depth / weight of X is …
  • How high is it? How much does it weigh? What is its area?
  • How many + countable; how much + uncountable.
  • two million, three thousand (no -s after a number).
