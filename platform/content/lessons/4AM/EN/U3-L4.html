Modals: should, must, may, might

Goal: To use the modal verbs should, must, may and might correctly to give advice, express obligation and necessity, ask permission and talk about possibility when discussing hopes and ambitions.

The idea in two lines: Modals are helping verbs that add a meaning (advice, obligation, possibility, permission) to the main verb. They never change form and are always followed by the base verb without to.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. General form: subject + modal + base verb (You should sleep early). No -s with he/she/it, no to, no do in questions and negatives: Should I go? / You mustn't shout.
  2. should / shouldn't: advice and recommendation. You should revise your lessons. You shouldn't waste time.
  3. must: strong obligation or necessity, often from the speaker or a rule. Pupils must respect the school rules. mustn't = prohibition: You mustn't cheat in the exam.
  4. may: possibility (about 50 %) or polite permission. It may snow tonight. May I come in?
  5. might: a weaker possibility, less sure than may. I might become an engineer, I'm not sure yet.
  6. In the future, must is replaced by will have to; in the past by had to.
  7. Short answers: Should I call him? – Yes, you should. / May I leave? – Yes, you may.

🟢 Examples

  1. Choose the right modal: Drivers ........ stop at the red light. Solution: Drivers must stop at the red light. Explanation: this is a law, a strong obligation.
  2. Nadia wants to become a pilot. Give her advice. Solution: She should study maths and physics seriously, and she shouldn't give up her dream.
  3. Will Amine join the trip? (he is not sure) Solution: He might join us, but he hasn't decided yet. Explanation: might shows an uncertain possibility.

🟠 Common BEM mistakes

  • Adding to: You must to go ✗ → You must go ✓.
  • Adding -s: She shoulds ✗; He musts ✗.
  • Confusing mustn't (prohibition) with don't have to (no obligation): You don't have to come = it is not necessary.
  • Using do in questions: Do you should...? ✗ → Should you...? ✓.

🟡 Quick review

  • Modal + base verb, no -s, no to, no do.
  • should = advice; must = obligation; mustn't = prohibition.
  • may = possibility or permission; might = weaker possibility.
  • Past of must = had to; future = will have to.
  • Question: modal + subject + verb (May I...?).
