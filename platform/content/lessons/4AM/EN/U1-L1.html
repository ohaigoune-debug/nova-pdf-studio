Describing landmarks: adjectives and adjective order

Goal: By the end of this lesson, you will be able to describe a landmark with several adjectives placed in the correct order, as in "a magnificent old Roman stone theatre".

The idea in two lines: Adjectives describe nouns and never change their form in English (one old tower, two old towers). When we use more than one adjective, they follow a fixed order: opinion first, then size, age, shape, colour, origin, material and purpose.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. An adjective gives information about a noun: tall, old, famous, stone. It has no plural form and no gender: two tall minarets, not "talls".
  2. Adjectives come before the noun (a famous mosque) or after the verb be and verbs like look, seem (The mosque is famous).
  3. The order of adjectives is: opinion, size, age, shape, colour, origin, material, purpose, then the noun. Remember the phrase "OSAShCOMP".
  4. Opinion adjectives (wonderful, impressive, boring) always come first because they express what we think, not a fact.
  5. Adjectives of origin (nationality) take a capital letter: Algerian, Egyptian, Chinese.
  6. Between two adjectives of the same type we use and: a red and white flag. Between different types, no comma: a tall old tower.
  7. Useful landmark adjectives: ancient, historic, impressive, enormous, sacred, well-preserved.

🟢 Examples

  1. Put in order: mosque / white / impressive / Ottoman. Answer: an impressive white Ottoman mosque. Explanation: opinion (impressive) → colour (white) → origin (Ottoman) → noun.
  2. Put in order: walls / stone / huge / old. Answer: huge old stone walls. Explanation: size (huge) → age (old) → material (stone). The adjectives stay singular even though "walls" is plural.
  3. Describe the Casbah of Algiers in one sentence. Answer: The Casbah is a fascinating old Algerian quarter with narrow winding streets. Explanation: opinion → age → origin; then size (narrow) before shape (winding).

🟠 Common BEM mistakes

  • Adding -s to adjectives: "two bigs towers". Adjectives never take a plural ending.
  • Putting the adjective after the noun as in French or Arabic: "a tower tall". In English the adjective comes before the noun.
  • Placing origin or material before opinion: "a stone beautiful bridge" instead of a beautiful stone bridge.
  • Forgetting the capital letter on nationality adjectives: "roman ruins" instead of Roman ruins.

🟡 Quick review

  • Adjective + noun; the adjective never changes its form.
  • Order: Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose.
  • Opinion always first; material comes just before purpose (or before the noun if there is no purpose adjective).
  • Nationality adjectives take a capital letter.
  • In your BEM paragraph, two or three well-ordered adjectives are enough.
