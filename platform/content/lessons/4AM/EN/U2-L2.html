Time markers and sequencers in a biography

Goal: By the end of this lesson, you will be able to organise the events of a biography in chronological order using time markers (in 1920, at the age of 12, two years later) and sequencers (first, then, finally).

The idea in two lines: A biography tells the main events of a person's life in order. Time markers say when something happened, and sequencers show the order of events so that the text is clear and well linked.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Time markers with a date: in 1954, in the 19th century, on 5 July 1962. Use in for years and centuries; on for full dates.
  2. Time markers with age or duration: at the age of ten, when he was 20, for three years, from 1950 to 1960.
  3. Relative time markers: two years later, a few years before, at that time.
  4. Sequencers order the events: first, then, next, after that, later, finally. They begin the sentence and take a comma.
  5. A biography follows a chronological order: birth → education → career → death (or present situation).
  6. Verbs are mostly in the past simple: was born, grew up, became, won, died.
  7. Key verbs: grow up, graduate from, get married, move to, be awarded, pass away.

🟢 Examples

  1. Choose the correct preposition: Ibn Badis was born … Constantine … 1889. Answer: in Constantine in 1889. Explanation: in before a city and a year; on only with a complete date.
  2. Link the events with sequencers: he studied in Algiers / he became a teacher / he published his first poem. Answer: First, he studied in Algiers. Then, he became a teacher. Finally, he published his first poem. Explanation: each sequencer opens the sentence and takes a comma.
  3. Rewrite with a relative time marker: She moved to London in 1960. She opened her studio in 1963. Answer: She moved to London in 1960. Three years later, she opened her studio. Explanation: 1963 − 1960 = 3.

🟠 Common BEM mistakes

  • Wrong preposition: "on 1962", "in 5 July 1962". Years take in; full dates take on.
  • Repeating then in every sentence. Vary: after that, later, finally.
  • Writing "he borned" or "he was borned". The correct form is he was born.
  • Mixing the order of events, for example the death before the career.

🟡 Quick review

  • in + year / century; on + full date; at the age of + number.
  • Sequencers: first, then, next, after that, later, finally (+ comma).
  • Relative markers: two years later, a few months before, at that time.
  • Order: birth → education → career → achievements → death.
  • Verbs in the past simple: was born, grew up, became, died.
