Expressing cause and result

Goal: To link two ideas by showing which one is the cause and which one is the result, using connectors such as because, since, as, so, therefore and as a result, especially when talking about the life of a famous figure.

The idea in two lines: A cause answers the question Why?; a result answers the question What happened then?. Some connectors introduce the cause (because, since, as, because of), and others introduce the result (so, therefore, that is why, as a result).

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. because / since / as + a full clause (subject + verb) introduce the cause: She became famous because she wrote great novels.
  2. because of / due to / thanks to + a noun or noun phrase (no verb): He succeeded thanks to his hard work. Use thanks to only for positive causes.
  3. so introduces the result and joins two clauses with a comma: He trained every day, so he won the race.
  4. therefore / consequently / as a result / that is why usually start a new sentence and are followed by a comma: He trained every day. Therefore, he won the race.
  5. The cause clause can come first or second. When it comes first, add a comma: Because he was curious, he read a lot.
  6. Use one connector per link: because ... so together is wrong.
  7. Test: the clause that answers Why? is the cause.

🟢 Examples

  1. Join: Avicenna studied medicine. He wrote The Canon of Medicine. Solution: Avicenna studied medicine, so he wrote The Canon of Medicine. / Avicenna wrote The Canon of Medicine because he had studied medicine.
  2. Rewrite with because of: The match was cancelled because it rained heavily. Solution: The match was cancelled because of the heavy rain. Explanation: because of needs a noun phrase, so rained becomes rain.
  3. Rewrite with therefore: Since Mouloud Feraoun loved his village, he described it in his books. Solution: Mouloud Feraoun loved his village. Therefore, he described it in his books.

🟠 Common BEM mistakes

  • Writing because of + clause: because of he was ill ✗ → because he was ill ✓ or because of his illness ✓.
  • Putting the result after because: the clause after because must always be the cause.
  • Forgetting the comma before so and after Therefore.
  • Using thanks to for a negative cause: He failed thanks to laziness ✗ → because of ✓.

🟡 Quick review

  • Cause connectors: because, since, as (+ clause); because of, due to, thanks to (+ noun).
  • Result connectors: so, therefore, consequently, as a result, that is why.
  • One connector per link: never because ... so.
  • Comma before so; comma after Therefore / As a result.
  • Ask Why? to find the cause, What then? to find the result.
