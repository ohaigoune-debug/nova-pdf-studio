Used to for past habits

Goal: By the end of this lesson, you will be able to talk about past habits and past states that are no longer true using used to + base form, in affirmative, negative and interrogative sentences.

The idea in two lines: Used to describes something that happened regularly or was true in the past but is not true now: People used to travel by camel across the Sahara. It has one form for all persons and is followed by the base form of the verb.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Affirmative: subject + used to + base form. My grandfather used to work in the fields. The form never changes.
  2. Negative: didn't use to + base form (use, no -d). She didn't use to like history.
  3. Question: Did + subject + use to + base form? Did people use to write with feathers?
  4. Past habit: a repeated action that has stopped. Picasso used to paint every day.
  5. Past state: a situation that is no longer true. This building used to be a palace; now it is a museum.
  6. Not for a single event: for one action at a definite time, use the past simple: He wrote his first poem in 1930.
  7. Contrast words: but now, nowadays, any more, no longer. She used to live in Oran, but now she lives in Annaba.

🟢 Examples

  1. Rewrite with used to: As a child, Charlie Chaplin performed in music halls in London. Answer: As a child, Charlie Chaplin used to perform in music halls in London. Explanation: a repeated past activity that stopped.
  2. Make the sentence negative: My parents used to have a television. Answer: My parents didn't use to have a television. Explanation: with didn't, the -d of used disappears.
  3. Ask a question: … your grandmother … (tell) you stories at night? Answer: Did your grandmother use to tell you stories at night? Explanation: Did + subject + use to + base form.

🟠 Common BEM mistakes

  • Writing "use to" in the affirmative: "He use to play". Write used to.
  • Writing "didn't used to" or "Did he used to". After did, write use to.
  • A past verb after used to: "used to went" instead of used to go.
  • Using used to for a present habit: "Nowadays I used to study every evening." Present habits take the present simple.

🟡 Quick review

  • used to + base form = past habit or past state, not true now.
  • Same form for all subjects: I / he / we used to.
  • Negative: didn't use to; question: Did … use to … ?
  • Single dated event → past simple, not used to.
  • Often with: but now, nowadays, any more, no longer.
