Comparatives and superlatives

Objective: To compare people, things and periods of time (past and present) using comparative and superlative forms of adjectives.

The idea in two lines: We use the comparative to compare two things and the superlative to put one thing at the top of a group of three or more. The form depends on the length of the adjective.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Short adjectives (one syllable): add -er / -est: old → older → the oldest.
  2. Spelling: double the final consonant after one short vowel (big → bigger); change -y to -i (easy → easier → the easiest).
  3. Long adjectives (most two-syllable adjectives not ending in -y, and all adjectives of three syllables or more): more / the most: modern → more modern → the most modern.
  4. Irregular forms: good → better → the best; bad → worse → the worst; far → farther/further → the farthest/furthest.
  5. Equality: as + adjective + as; inferiority: not as … as or less … than.
  6. Comparative is followed by than; the superlative takes the and often in / of.

🟢 Examples

  1. Compare: "Life today / life in the past (easy)". Answer: Life today is easier than life in the past. Explanation: two-syllable adjective ending in -y takes -er.
  2. Complete: "Smartphones are (useful) invention of our time." Answer: Smartphones are the most useful invention of our time.
  3. Rewrite with as … as: "Old houses were less comfortable than modern ones." Answer: Old houses were not as comfortable as modern ones.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Double comparison: more easier ✗ → easier ✓.
  • Using then instead of than.
  • Forgetting the before the superlative: It is most modern city ✗.
  • Writing gooder or badder instead of the irregular forms better / worse.

🟡 Quick review

  • Short: -er than / the -est.
  • Long: more … than / the most ….
  • good–better–best; bad–worse–worst.
  • Equality: as … as; inferiority: not as … as.
  • Never mix -er and more.
