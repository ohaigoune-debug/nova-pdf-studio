Modals: must, should, ought to

Objective: To express obligation, prohibition and advice using the modals must, should and ought to, for example when talking about peace and good citizenship.

The idea in two lines: Modals add meaning to the main verb. Must expresses strong obligation (often from the speaker), while should and ought to give advice or say what is right to do.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Form: modal + base form, with no -s in the third person: She must leave, not She musts leaves.
  2. Must: strong obligation or necessity: Citizens must obey the law.
  3. Mustn't: prohibition (it is forbidden): You mustn't use violence.
  4. Should / ought to: advice or what is morally right: We should / ought to solve conflicts peacefully. Ought is followed by to.
  5. Negatives: shouldn't, ought not to; questions: Should I …?
  6. Have to: obligation from outside (rules); don't have to = no obligation, which is different from mustn't.

🟢 Examples

  1. Give advice to a friend who is always fighting: Answer: You should control your anger and talk calmly. Explanation: advice, not an order.
  2. Express a school rule about cheating: Answer: Pupils mustn't cheat in exams. Explanation: prohibition.
  3. Correct the mistake: "Nations ought respect human rights." Answer: Nations ought to respect human rights.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Adding to after must or should: must to go ✗ → must go ✓.
  • Forgetting to after ought.
  • Confusing mustn't (forbidden) with don't have to (not necessary).
  • Adding -s or -ed to the modal or to the verb after it.

🟡 Quick review

  • Modal + base form, no -s.
  • must = strong obligation; mustn't = prohibition.
  • should = ought to = advice.
  • ought + to + verb.
  • mustn't ≠ don't have to.
