Future forms

Objective: Choose the right future form (will, be going to, present continuous, future continuous, future perfect) to make predictions and talk about plans.

In two lines: English has no single future tense. The form we choose depends on whether we predict, decide, plan, or describe an action in progress or completed by a future time.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. will + base verb: predictions based on opinion, and decisions made at the moment of speaking: Robots will do housework.
  2. be going to: intentions and predictions based on present evidence: Look at the clouds! It's going to rain.
  3. Present continuous: fixed arrangements: We are visiting the science museum on Sunday.
  4. Future continuous (will be + V-ing): an action in progress at a future time: This time next year, I will be studying physics.
  5. Future perfect (will have + past participle): an action completed before a future time, often with by: By 2050, scientists will have found new energy sources.
  6. After when, as soon as, before, until we use the present, not will.

🟢 Examples

  1. Complete: By the end of this century, people (build) cities on the Moon. Answer: will have built. Explanation: completed before a future time (by).
  2. Complete: At 8 tomorrow, we (watch) the documentary. Answer: will be watching. Explanation: in progress at a precise future moment.
  3. Correct: I will call you when I will arrive. Answer: I will call you when I arrive.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Using will after time conjunctions (when, as soon as).
  • Forgetting be in going to: I going to travel.
  • Confusing future continuous and future perfect when the sentence contains by.
  • Writing will have finish instead of the past participle finished.

🟡 Quick review

  • will: prediction or quick decision.
  • going to: plan or evidence now.
  • will be + V-ing: in progress then.
  • will have + V3: finished by then.
  • Time clauses take the present simple.
