Wishes and regrets

Objective: Express wishes about the present and regrets about the past using I wish and If only, and choose the correct tense after them.

In two lines: After wish / if only we move the verb one tense back. The past simple shows a wish about now; the past perfect shows a regret about the past.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Present wish: subject + wish + past simple: I wish I had more friends. (Fact: I don't have many friends.)
  2. With the verb be, we can use were for all persons: I wish he were here.
  3. Past regret: subject + wish + past perfect: She wishes she hadn't lied. (Fact: she lied.)
  4. If only has the same structure but is stronger and more emotional: If only I had listened!
  5. wish + would expresses annoyance or a desire for someone else to change: I wish you would help me.
  6. Ability: use could for a present wish: I wish I could swim.

🟢 Examples

  1. Rewrite: I'm sorry I didn't visit my grandmother. Answer: I wish I had visited my grandmother. Explanation: regret about the past, so past perfect with opposite meaning.
  2. Rewrite: It's a pity we live far from our neighbours. Answer: If only we didn't live far from our neighbours. Explanation: present situation, so past simple.
  3. Complete: My brother never shares his things. I wish he (share) them. Answer: would share.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Keeping the original tense: I wish I have a car instead of I wish I had a car.
  • Forgetting to reverse the meaning: I didn't help becomes I wish I had helped, not hadn't helped.
  • Using I wish I would… about yourself; use could or the past simple instead.
  • Using the past simple for past regrets: I wish I studied yesterday is wrong.

🟡 Quick review

  • Now: wish + past simple / could.
  • Past: wish + had + past participle.
  • Someone else's behaviour: wish + would.
  • If only = a stronger wish, same tenses.
  • Always reverse positive and negative.
