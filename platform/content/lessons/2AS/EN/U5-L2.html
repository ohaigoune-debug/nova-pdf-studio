Narrative tenses

Objective: To narrate a story or a news event in the past by combining the past simple, past continuous and past perfect in a clear order.

The idea in two lines: Narrative tenses work together: the past simple carries the main events, the past continuous sets the background, and the past perfect goes back to an earlier action.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Past simple: the main, completed events of the story, in the order they happened.
  2. Past continuous (was / were + -ing): background, description, or an action interrupted by another.
  3. Past perfect (had + past participle): an action that happened before another past action.
  4. Time connectors: when, while, as, as soon as, after, before, by the time, then, suddenly, eventually.
  5. After / before already show the order, so the past perfect is sometimes optional with them.
  6. Story structure: setting (background) → events → climax → ending.

🟢 Examples

  1. Put the verbs in the right tense: "When the police (arrive), the thief (already / escape)." Answer: When the police arrived, the thief had already escaped. Explanation: the escape happened first.
  2. "The sun (shine) and the children (play) when the storm (start)." Answer: The sun was shining and the children were playing when the storm started.
  3. Join with by the time: "The firemen came. The house burned down before that." Answer: By the time the firemen came, the house had burned down.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Using the past simple for the earlier action when the order is unclear.
  • Wrong form of the past perfect: had went ✗ → had gone ✓.
  • Writing the whole story in the past continuous.
  • Switching to the present in the middle of the narrative.

🟡 Quick review

  • Main events ⇒ past simple.
  • Background ⇒ past continuous.
  • Earlier action ⇒ had + past participle.
  • Connectors make the order clear.
  • Stay in the past from start to end.
