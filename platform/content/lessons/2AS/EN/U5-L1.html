Reported speech

Objective: To report statements, questions and orders in indirect speech, applying the changes of tense, pronouns and time expressions.

The idea in two lines: In reported speech we tell what someone said without quoting their exact words. When the reporting verb is in the past (said, told, asked), the tense usually moves one step back.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Statements: said (that) / told someone (that) + clause: "I am tired." → He said (that) he was tired.
  2. Backshift: present → past; past and present perfect → past perfect; will → would; can → could; may → might.
  3. Pronouns and possessives change according to the speaker: I → he / she, my → his / her.
  4. Yes/no questions: asked + if / whether + subject + verb (no question form): She asked if I liked news.
  5. Wh- questions: asked + wh-word + subject + verb: He asked where I lived.
  6. Orders and requests: told / asked + object + (not) to + base form: She told me not to shout.

🟢 Examples

  1. "I will write the article tomorrow," the journalist said. Answer: The journalist said (that) he would write the article the next day.
  2. "Did you read the news?" Sara asked me. Answer: Sara asked me if I had read the news. Explanation: past simple → past perfect, no auxiliary did.
  3. "Close the window," the teacher said to us. Answer: The teacher told us to close the window.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Keeping the question form: He asked where did I live ✗ → where I lived ✓.
  • Using said me ✗: we say told me or said to me.
  • Forgetting to change pronouns and time expressions.
  • Keeping quotation marks or the question mark in reported speech.

🟡 Quick review

  • One tense back after a past reporting verb.
  • Questions: if / whether or wh- + normal word order.
  • Orders: told + object + (not) to + verb.
  • Change pronouns, time and place words.
