Quantifiers

Objective: Use quantifiers (some, any, much, many, a lot of, few, a few, little, a little) correctly with countable and uncountable nouns.

In two lines: Quantifiers tell us how much or how many. The right choice depends on whether the noun is countable or uncountable and whether the sentence is positive, negative or a question.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Countable nouns have a plural (jobs, banks); uncountable nouns do not (money, information, advice).
  2. some in positive sentences and offers; any in negatives and questions: We don't have any stock.
  3. many + plural countable; much + uncountable, mostly in negatives and questions.
  4. a lot of / lots of go with both types, mainly in positive sentences.
  5. a few / a little = a small but sufficient amount (positive idea).
  6. few / little = almost none (negative idea): Few people bought the product.

🟢 Examples

  1. Choose: The company doesn't have (much / many) money. Answer: much. Explanation: money is uncountable.
  2. Choose: Luckily, I have (a few / few) good ideas, so I can start a small business. Answer: a few. Explanation: positive meaning, enough to start.
  3. Correct: Can you give me an advice? Answer: Can you give me some advice? (or a piece of advice)

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Pluralising uncountable nouns: informations, advices, equipments.
  • Using much with countable nouns: much customers.
  • Confusing few (negative) and a few (positive).
  • Using some in negative sentences: I don't have some time.

🟡 Quick review

  • many / a few / few + countable.
  • much / a little / little + uncountable.
  • some (positive), any (negative, question).
  • a lot of + both types.
  • a few / a little = enough; few / little = not enough.
