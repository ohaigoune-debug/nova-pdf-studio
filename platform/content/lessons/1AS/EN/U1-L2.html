Asking for and giving information (wh-questions)

Goal: To ask for information with the right wh-word and the correct word order, and to answer such questions with short, precise information.

The idea in two lines: A wh-question starts with a question word (what, where, when, who, why, how...) followed by an auxiliary and the subject: Wh + auxiliary + subject + verb? The answer gives exactly the information the wh-word asks for.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Word order: Wh-word + auxiliary (do / does / is / are / can...) + subject + main verb. Example: When do you start school?
  2. With the verb "be" there is no "do": Where are you from? What is your name?
  3. Who / What as subject: no auxiliary is needed. Who teaches English? What happened?
  4. How + adjective / adverb: How old, how far, how long, how often, how many (countable), how much (uncountable).
  5. Which asks for a choice from a known set: Which bus goes to the centre?
  6. Whose asks about possession: Whose bag is this?
  7. Answering: give the information asked for, in a short or full sentence: Where do you live? – In Oran. / I live in Oran.

🟢 Examples

  1. Ask about the underlined words: My sister studies medicine. Answer: What does your sister study? Explanation: "medicine" is a thing → what; the subject is third person → does; the main verb returns to its base form.
  2. Ask about the underlined words: Karim won the race. Answer: Who won the race? Explanation: the question is about the subject, so no auxiliary is added and the verb keeps its past form.
  3. The train leaves at 7 o'clock. Answer: When does the train leave? (or: What time does the train leave?)

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Forgetting the auxiliary: "Where you live?" instead of "Where do you live?"
  • Keeping the -s or the past form after the auxiliary: "Where does she lives?" / "When did he went?"
  • Confusing how many (books) with how much (money).
  • Using "what" for a person: "What teaches you English?" instead of "Who teaches you English?"

🟡 Quick review

  • Wh-word + auxiliary + subject + base verb?
  • With "be": Wh-word + am / is / are + subject?
  • Who / What as subject → no do / does / did.
  • How many = countable, how much = uncountable.
  • Find the kind of information (place, time, person, reason) and pick the matching wh-word.
