Reporting an experiment and its findings

Goal: To write a short report on a simple experiment with the standard sections (aim, materials, procedure, results, conclusion), using the passive voice, sequencers and cause-and-result connectors.

The idea in two lines: An experiment report answers four questions: what we wanted to find out, what we did, what we observed, and what it means. The style is impersonal: the passive voice focuses on the actions and findings, not on the people.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Aim: one sentence with "to + verb": The aim of this experiment was to find out whether salt changes the boiling point of water.
  2. Materials: a simple list of the equipment and substances used (a beaker, a thermometer, water, salt).
  3. Procedure: the steps in order, in the past simple passive with sequencers: First, 200 ml of water was poured into a beaker. Then it was heated...
  4. Results: what was observed or measured, often with numbers: It was noticed that... / The temperature rose to...
  5. Conclusion / findings: what the results prove, in the present simple with cause and result connectors: The results show that... ; therefore, ...
  6. Impersonal style: avoid "I" and "we"; prefer the passive. Useful reporting verbs: show, reveal, indicate, prove, confirm.

🟢 Examples

  1. Write the procedure from these notes: pour water / add 2 spoons of salt / heat / note the temperature. Answer: First, water was poured into a beaker. Then, 2 spoons of salt were added. After that, the mixture was heated. Finally, the boiling temperature was noted. Explanation: past passive + a different sequencer for each step.
  2. Write a conclusion for: pure water boiled at 100°C; salt water boiled at 102°C (to be checked). Answer: The results show that salt water boils at a higher temperature than pure water. Therefore, salt raises the boiling point of water. Explanation: reporting verb + present simple + result connector.
  3. Make this sentence impersonal: We saw that the ice melted faster in the sun. Answer: It was observed that the ice melted faster in the sun.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Writing the procedure in the imperative (Pour the water...) instead of the past passive.
  • Using "I" and "we" throughout the report instead of the impersonal style.
  • Mixing results (what was seen) with the conclusion (what it means).
  • Forgetting sequencers, so the steps have no clear order.

🟡 Quick review

  • Aim → Materials → Procedure → Results → Conclusion.
  • Procedure: past passive + first / then / after that / finally.
  • Results: it was observed / noticed that...
  • Conclusion: the results show that... + therefore / so.
  • No "I / we": keep it impersonal.
