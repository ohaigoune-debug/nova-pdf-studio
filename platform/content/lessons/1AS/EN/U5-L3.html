Comparatives and superlatives

Goal: To form comparatives and superlatives of short and long adjectives, to use the structures as … as and (not) as … as, and to compare animals, places and environmental problems correctly.

The idea in two lines: A comparative compares two things (bigger than, more dangerous than). A superlative picks one out of a group (the biggest, the most dangerous). The length of the adjective decides the form.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Short adjectives (one syllable): add -er / -est: cold → colder than → the coldest.
  2. Spelling: one vowel + one consonant → double the consonant (hot → hotter, hottest); final -e → add -r / -st (large → larger, largest); final -y → -ier / -iest (happy → happier, happiest).
  3. Long adjectives (two syllables or more, except those ending in -y): more / most + adjective: beautiful → more beautiful than → the most beautiful.
  4. Irregular forms must be learnt: good / better / best, bad / worse / worst, far / farther / farthest, little / less / least, many / much / more / most.
  5. Than follows a comparative; the comes before a superlative, often with "in" (in the world) or "of" (of all).
  6. Equality: as + adjective + as; inequality: not as (so) + adjective + as. "A cheetah is not as heavy as a lion."
  7. Modifiers: much / far / a lot + comparative (much hotter); by far + superlative.

🟢 Examples

  1. Complete: "The Sahara is ___ (large) desert in Africa." Answer: the largest. Explanation: one thing out of a group, short adjective, final -e so only -st is added.
  2. Complete: "City air is ___ (polluted) than mountain air." Answer: more polluted. Explanation: three syllables, so more + adjective; than is already there.
  3. Rewrite keeping the meaning: "The Nile is longer than the Niger." → "The Niger is not ___." Answer: The Niger is not as long as the Nile. Explanation: not as … as expresses the same comparison from the other side.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Double forms: "more bigger", "the most hottest".
  • Writing "that" or "then" instead of than after a comparative.
  • Forgetting the before a superlative or the doubled consonant (biger, hoter).
  • Using "gooder" or "badder" instead of better / worse.

🟡 Quick review

  • Short adjective: -er than / the -est.
  • Long adjective: more … than / the most ….
  • -y → -ier / -iest; double the final consonant after one short vowel.
  • good → better → best; bad → worse → worst.
  • as … as = equal; not as … as = less.
