Writing a formal letter and an email

Goal: To write a short formal letter and a formal email with the correct layout, opening and closing formulas, and a polite, clear register.

The idea in two lines: A formal letter or email follows a fixed structure: greeting, purpose, details, request, polite closing. The language is formal: no contractions, no slang, polite full sentences.

🔵 Rules and concepts

  1. Layout of a letter: your address and the date at the top right, the receiver's name and address on the left, then the greeting.
  2. Greeting: "Dear Sir / Madam," when you do not know the name; "Dear Mr / Mrs / Ms + surname," when you do.
  3. Closing: "Yours faithfully," after Dear Sir / Madam; "Yours sincerely," after a name. In an email: "Best regards," or "Kind regards,".
  4. Opening sentence states the purpose: I am writing to apply for... / to ask for information about... / to complain about...
  5. Body paragraphs: one idea per paragraph; details, then the request: I would be grateful if you could...
  6. Final sentence: I look forward to hearing from you. / Thank you for your attention.
  7. Email specifics: a clear subject line, no postal addresses, short paragraphs, same formal register.

🟢 Examples

  1. Opening of a letter asking for information about a language course: Answer: "Dear Sir / Madam, I am writing to ask for information about the summer English course advertised on your website. Could you please tell me the dates and the fees?" Explanation: unknown receiver → Dear Sir / Madam; purpose in the first sentence; polite question.
  2. Closing an email to a teacher you know by name: Answer: "I look forward to your reply. Kind regards, Yasmine Hadj, 1AS2." Explanation: final polite sentence, email closing, full name and class.
  3. Make formal: "Hey, can u send me the papers asap?" Answer: "I would be grateful if you could send me the documents as soon as possible." Explanation: no abbreviations, no slang, polite request form.

🟠 Common BAC mistakes

  • Using contractions and informal words (I'm, can't, hi, thanks a lot) in a formal letter.
  • Mixing closings: "Dear Sir, ... Yours sincerely" instead of "Yours faithfully".
  • Forgetting the subject line in an email or the date in a letter.
  • Writing one long paragraph with no clear purpose sentence.

🟡 Quick review

  • Letter: addresses + date → greeting → purpose → details → request → closing → name.
  • Dear Sir / Madam ↔ Yours faithfully; Dear Mr X ↔ Yours sincerely.
  • Email: subject line, short paragraphs, Best / Kind regards.
  • Formal = no contractions, no slang, polite forms (I would be grateful if...).
  • End with: I look forward to hearing from you.
